# Breakup: The town underneath

What Kettle Lake does that nobody says. Decided on October 12, from two
references: **_Horses_** (Santa Ragione), where the horror is a routine
everyone treats as normal, and **_The Devil in Ohio_**, a sect hidden inside an
ordinary community, with a mark on the body and kind adults who are all in it.

This is the folk horror strand (*it's the town*) at its most human. **Nothing
here is supernatural.** It all obeys the rule of two explanations
(`pillars.md`): the second explanation is usually MC's mind.

---

## How it's written

- **The disturbing thing is normal to everyone but MC.** Nobody explains it,
  nobody lowers their voice. It comes up in the same sentence as the weather.
  Only MC and the voices react.
- **About one detail a day,** in passing, never explained in the same scene,
  never escalated in the scene it appears in.
- **Never called a cult on screen.** No robes, no chanting, until the frenzy in
  February. Until then it's a town with habits.
- **The kindness and the sect are the same thing.** The people who feed MC are
  the people who will walk MC to the shore.
- **Daniel doesn't make it, he finds it.** A mark, a ritual, a scapegoat,
  already in place. He gives it a voice (`daniel.md`).

## The Keeper's dog: Mrs. Hollis

The biggest secret in the town's habits, and the game's best horror image.

- **Where it comes from.** Abraham's wife had a dog, and a bowl. She died in
  the spring of 1989 (still open). The legend (`story.md`, "The dog") says the
  dog is whoever the shop took last, come up at night, hungry. The congregation
  made the legend flesh: every winter since 1989, someone is chosen at church
  to be **the Keeper's dog**. They come in by the back door after midnight,
  climb the stairs on their hands and knees, and eat from the bowl. Abraham fed
  them every night for ten years. Rule five is him asking a stranger to keep
  doing it.
- **Who it is this winter.** Mrs. Hollis. The lake took her husband three weeks
  before MC came, and her nephew on day two. The penitent is usually someone the
  lake has taken from. She believes kneeling keeps it from taking a third.
- **Why it fits her.** Long gray hair, worn in a bun by day. The scratches on
  the steel door are higher than a dog standing, right for a woman on her knees
  reaching up. By day she feeds the Keeper (the hotdish); by night she eats from
  the Keeper's bowl. The town feeds the lamb; the lamb feeds the dog.
- **What it does to the choice.** Feeding the dog is mercy toward someone MC
  knows. Leaving the bowl empty means an old woman waited all night on cold
  stairs. The bowl set back exactly on the line: she watches how MC does it,
  because she's careful. Keeping faith with the Hollises (`forks.md`) is
  feeding her, every night, and never saying so.
- **The second explanation** stays: MC sleepwalking, frayed, putting the kind
  woman's face on the thing on the stairs. The February scene can sit inside
  the blur.

How it surfaces, never fully confirmed:

| When | What |
|---|---|
| October | Gray hair, the bowl, the scratches (built). Day three: the knees of her good slacks worn shiny; her eyes go to the foot of the stairs; "You're thinner than Thomas was." She watches MC eat the way you'd watch a dog eat. |
| November | At church she kneels lower than everyone else. Her knuckles are calloused, and Appraisal notices. |
| December | Leaving a dish: *"You line it up so neatly. Abraham never did."* She means the bowl. MC never told anyone about the bowl. |
| February | The one time MC sees it: after midnight, at the bottom of the stairs, a figure on all fours, long gray hair loose in the neon. It doesn't look up. In the morning she's at the counter with a hotdish. If she ever says one line about it: *"Somebody has to."* |

## The mark

- A small old burn on the inside of the wrist: **four short lines, crossed.**
  The shape of the four spruce poles lashed together on the shore, waiting under
  their tarp for the ice.
- Given at church, young. Most of the congregation has one; nobody shows it,
  nobody hides it.
- **Built:** Mrs. Hollis (day one, rubbing her eyes), Ezra (day two, counting
  out his six dollars), the lid of the jar of milk teeth.
- **Later:** in Ruth's bar mirror (does she have one? open); by February MC
  notices who *doesn't*. Olstad doesn't. The Reverend doesn't, and that's why
  the congregation can turn on him.
- **The second explanation:** a burn from a stove, a hot plate, a wood stove.
  Old people in cold places have burns.

## The children's rhyme

A skipping rhyme that counts the rules, one to eight, and then the Keeper:

> One, the window. Two, the warm. Three, the price...
> Eight, the glass, and nine's the Keeper, in he goes.

- **Built:** day three, at the bus stop across the road (the quiet afternoon, or
  the boy with the fry bread).
- **Later:** June's Thanksgiving pageant at the school; a child plays the
  Keeper, in a flannel shirt. The boy on day three: *"Is it true you're not
  allowed to leave?"*

## Fattening the lamb

- The gifts never stop: hotdish, fry bread, coffee, birch for the stove.
- Mrs. Hollis: *"You're thinner than Thomas was. He filled out by Christmas"*
  (built, day three).
- **Later:** at church, the Keeper is weighed on the old grain scale in the
  vestry, "for the records". The numbers are in a book, one page per winter.

## Things the town leaves with the shop

Pawned, held, logged in the ledger like anything else, priced by Appraisal,
unbearable to Paranoia.

- **Built:** the **jar of milk teeth** (back shelf, tag 0398, "For the church,
  when asked"): every tooth in a twist of paper with initials and a year, 1919
  to 1998. C.H. '86 is Cody Hollis. E.L. '32 is Ezra Lund. The mark on the lid.
- **Later, one at a time:** a braid of hair, "pawned, no loan"; a child's mitten
  with a ticket pinned to it; a Bible with one verse cut out of every page
  (Leviticus 16 is the last one left whole).

## The coat (decided, week two)

The answer to the weighing, the doorframe, the braid and the teeth: **the
congregation sews the Keeper a coat** every winter. Long, brown wool, to the
ankles, made by the Ladies' Circle on Thursdays at the Lunds'. Height for the
length, weight for the cut ("He filled out by Christmas": they let it out).
The lining holds the town: braids sewn in at the hem, children's first teeth
in little pockets stitched shut, widows' hairpins in the cuffs. The Keeper
wears it onto the ice at breakup and carries the town's children, their sins
and their bad luck, out with them. Leviticus 16, made warm. It's sewn with
love. It's the best coat in Kettle Lake. **Built:** June's drawings, the brown
thread, the Lunds' window, the coat under a sheet in the vestry
(`week_two.md`).

**Under the ice** (week three): the first clear week of new ice shows what the
coat is for. Out past the lanterns at the first-ice party, Thomas, last
winter's Keeper, face up in his coat, a yard down. The town skates over him.

## The church's own wall

- Ruth's cellar has a second Polaroid of every Keeper (`forks.md`). At the
  church they hang in the vestry with the date each one went under, and a
  pencilled height mark beside each, like a kitchen doorframe.
- MC's is there by November. No date yet.

## Small habits

- **Built:** Ezra knocks twice on the shop's door frame on his way out. At the
  Gleaner on Friday, a glass poured at last call that nobody drinks.
- **Later:** everyone knocks twice; "God keep you", and they don't mean God; an
  extra place set at the Thanksgiving potluck, facing the lake.
