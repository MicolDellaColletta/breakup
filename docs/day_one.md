# Breakup: Day one

First full day after the prologue. Draft for discussion: the structure and the
systems are proposals, the customers are placeholders to rewrite.

Inspirations: Strange Horticulture / Strange Antiquities (counter, customers,
identifying items), Disco Elysium and I Was a Teenage Exocolonist (voices,
painterly scenes, time as the main resource).

---

## The shape of a day

| Time (rough) | Part | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 7:00 AM | Morning, apartment | Wake scene (from the prologue). The dog bowl. Consequences of last night's rules. |
| 9:00 AM | Open the shop | It's still dark: in October the sun rises around 9 and sets around 6:30. |
| 9 AM to 5 PM | Shop hours | 4 customers, one at a time. Each one takes time. |
| Afternoon | The Trooper | Not a customer. Comes in between customers. |
| ~5 PM | Close the shop | Rule 1: windows closed before dark. |
| Evening | Town map | Travel to the pub (day one). Travel costs time. |
| Night | Back at the shop | Rules, exploring, main plot. Then sleep. |

**Time is the only currency.** No separate energy. Distance costs hours, and
the rules are about time ("before dark", "at night", "after midnight"), so every
trip is a choice between going somewhere and being home when the rules start.

## The counter (the shop's home screen)

The player stands behind the counter facing the front windows (see `layout.md`).
From here they can:

- **Look at the shop floor:** items for sale.
- **Turn to the back shelves:** pawned items, held, not for sale.
- **Open the counter drawers** (hidden ones come later).
- **Read the ledger:** every item, its price, who brought it in.
- **The phone and the register** are here too.

Customers come to the counter. Everything else happens around them.

## Customers

Every customer is a decision, not a transaction.

**Customers who buy don't name the item, they describe it.** The player works
out which item they mean from the shelves, the item descriptions and the ledger,
then hands it over or refuses. Wrong item, right item, or refusing all matter
later.

**Customers who sell or pawn** bring something in. The player decides whether
to take it and at what price. This is where the rules get tested.

- **Pawn** = a loan. The item goes on the back shelves; the customer can come
  back for it. Creates callbacks later.
- **Sell** = final. The item goes out front for sale.

### Day one: 4 customers (placeholders)

1. **Buyer: someone who knew the old keeper.** Asks for "the usual" and expects
   you to know what that is. The ledger has the answer, if you look. Tests
   whether you can pass as the keeper.
2. **Buyer: a gift for someone who's gone.** "Something my husband would have
   wanted. He hunted. He'd have hated that I came here." Find the right item.
3. **Buyer: wants something specific and won't say why.** Leaves a strange
   feeling. Their item could be connected to the past (see Items).
4. **Seller: the warm item. Rule 2, "Don't buy anything that's still warm."**
   They put it on the counter. When you pick it up, it's warm. Buy it or not.

## The Trooper

Alaska State Trooper (the police outside towns in rural Alaska). The most
dangerous person in the game for someone living under a stolen name.

- **Calls you "John"** and watches how long it takes you to answer.
- **Knows too much, quietly.** "Saw a sedan come in late last night. One
  headlight dimmer than the other. Yours?" (The radio caller's words from the
  drive, said to your face.)
- Strange warnings that sound like small talk.
- **Invites you to the pub tonight** to meet the residents. This introduces the map.

## Items

Some items have properties beyond their price:

- **Connected to the past.** Handling them triggers a short memory with a choice
  inside it. That choice shapes the background (see below).
- **Unsettling.** Handling them frays MC. Being frayed makes the voices louder
  (more Paranoia, more ???) and makes strange things more likely.

"Frayed" is a separate thing from the insanity background: it's a state MC can
be in, and insanity is one way of reading it.

## Backgrounds

MC's past is shaped by choices, not picked from a menu. Four directions:

- **Guilt**: escaping something they did.
- **Witness**: they saw something.
- **Debt**: they owe someone.
- **Insanity**: lost time, gaps in memory; they don't know why they ran.
  (Something specific, so it doesn't turn into "it was all in their head".)

The background grows out of which voices MC listens to (see `voices.md`), not
from separate tags. The prologue's hints are written to fit all four.

## Money

Careful: money should create pressure and consequences, never a spreadsheet.

- **Few, round numbers.** $5, $40, $200.
- **No haggling slider.** Price offers are choices: lowball, fair, generous.
  Each says something about MC. "Fair" keeps rule 3.
- **No game over from running out**, at least early on.
- **It starts almost empty.** The register drawer is open and empty (prologue).
  MC has a little cash of their own; why depends on the background.
- Wages: weekly, in cash (the job offer). Who pays them?

## Evening: the town map

After closing, the player opens the map of Kettle Lake and the area around it.

- Each place costs travel time there and back, plus time spent there.
- Far places use up the evening; near ones allow two or three stops.
- **Day one: small map.** The pub (the Trooper's invitation), maybe one more
  place, so the player learns how it works.
- The pub: meet the residents. (Its own doc later.)

## Night: back at the shop

After the map, or straight after closing if the player stays in.

- **Rules at night:** windows before dark, radio on, back door unlocked (walking
  past the freezer to check), feed the dog.
- **Exploring:** drawers, the office, the hallway, the locked door.
- **Main plot beats.**

## The dog

Never seen. Rule 5: "Feed the dog."

- **Prologue (proposed):** an optional object in the apartment kitchen, like the
  TV. "A dog bowl on the floor by the stove, licked clean. A bag of kibble, half
  full. No dog." Fill it or leave it. Not filling it (or never finding it)
  breaks the rule.
- **Every morning:** the bowl is empty.
- **Some nights:** scratching at a door. (Idea: from behind the locked door.)

## Building order

Each step can be played and tested before the next.

1. **Counter view + one customer, end to end** (the vertical slice): look
   around, find the item, hand it over or refuse.
2. Money and the ledger.
3. The other three customers, including the warm item.
4. The Trooper.
5. Voices shaping the background (see `voices.md`), once the mapping is decided.
6. Closing time, the map, the pub.
7. Night at the shop, then sleep into day two.
