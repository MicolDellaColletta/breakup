# Breakup: Themes

What the game is about, under the horror. Read this before writing a scene,
and check the scene against the list at the end.

---

## What it's about

**Emptiness and loneliness, and the mental illness that grows in them.**
Guilt, fear and desire. Not being able to say what you mean, or be heard.
Meaninglessness, and the meaning people make anyway. Destroying yourself,
and the chance to rebuild, which comes through suffering, not around it.

The title carries all of it. **Breakup** is the ice going out: the frozen
thing has to crack, and it's violent and it's spring. Nothing in this game
gets reborn without breaking first.

## Where each theme lives

The themes shouldn't be said. They should be in the things the player does.

| Theme | Where it already lives | What to keep doing |
|---|---|---|
| **Emptiness** | The dark shop at night. The empty register. The shelves of things people gave up. An empty bowl licked clean. A ledger line with no item (Cody). | Silence as a presence. Rooms that are too big. Things that should be there and aren't. |
| **Loneliness** | Nobody in the world knows where MC is. The thirteenth stool. Ten Polaroids. The radio as the only company. Ruth waiting for a call. Sam counting alone. | Every character is lonely in their own way. Company is always partial: through a radio, a counter, a phone. |
| **Mental illness** | The voices. Paranoia. Being frayed. The missing hours (insanity). The dog that is someone. | See "Writing it with care" below. |
| **Guilt** | Warmth. The guilt background. Walt's silver and Ida's name. Cody and the ice picks (you sold them, or you didn't). Ruth living off the Keepers. Abraham and the ram. | Choices whose cost lands on someone else. No clean options. |
| **Fear** | Paranoia, the main voice. The phone. The back door. Daniel. | Fear that protects and fear that destroys are the same voice. Paranoia keeps MC alive, and it's also what keeps him alone. |
| **Desire** | The romances. Daniel's "sweetheart". ??? wanting things for MC. Wanting to be John, to be anyone else. Walt wanting to stop saying a name. | **The weakest so far.** Day one and two have almost none. Desire should show up early and small: wanting to stay, wanting to be seen, wanting to be wanted, wanting the wrong thing. |
| **Not being able to communicate** | Rule four: don't speak first. The name nobody can say. The ink you can't read. The open line where nobody speaks. Voices only MC hears. The live microphone. | **Meaninglessness and meaning** | Layer 3: the lake doesn't care. Sam's numbers. The town's faith, which is folly and is also how they survive. ## Rebuilding and rebirth

The game must not only be about falling. It has to let MC (and the player)
rebuild, and the rebuilding has to cost something.

- **The rules are routine.** They're horror, and they're also structure: close
  the windows, feed the dog, keep the radio on. Keeping them is how MC comes back
  from frayed. That's how recovery often works: small repeated acts, kept on
  bad days, with relapses. The mechanic already says this. Keep it honest: one
  clean day helps a little; it never fixes everything.
- **Small comforts** (`fray=-1`): once or twice a week, a real moment of
  warmth that isn't a trick. A cup of coffee handed over without being asked.
  Someone remembering MC's order. Sam saying "I'd like you to be eleven for a
  while." They should be rare enough to matter.
- **Unsettling things** (`fray=+1`): also once or twice a week. Handling the
  warm silver. The bowl with the gray hairs. They should be rare enough not to
  become noise.
- **Breakup is rebirth.** The ice has to break for spring to come. The endings
  are different answers to whether MC breaks with it, or through it.

### The endings, as answers

| Ending | What it says |
|---|---|
| The season ends | Self-destruction. The emptiness wins. |
| Run | Escape without rebuilding. The guilt goes to whoever is left holding the keys. |
| The new owner | Meaning made by using other people. Abraham's answer. |
| Break the cycle | Rebuilding through suffering, with someone else. It costs. |
| The truth | Meaning found by facing the meaningless, with someone else. |
| Daniel | Desire as self-destruction. |
| Daniel, secret | Emptiness kept forever. |

**Gap:** every ending where MC rebuilds needs a romance. There should probably
be one where MC rebuilds **alone**, or with the town, or simply by getting
through the winter keeping faith with the rules and the people he's met. Call
it **Thaw**. To decide.

## Writing it with care

Mental illness is at the centre of this game, so it has to be written from the
inside, with respect, not used as spectacle.

- **The voices aren't a diagnosis.** They're a way of showing an inner life.
  Paranoia isn't "the crazy part": it's fear that has learned to keep MC alive,
  and it's also isolating him.
- **Being frayed should feel true to lived experience:** intrusive thoughts,
  bad advice from your own head, the world going flat, losing time. Not
  screaming and blood.
- **Mental illness is never what makes someone the monster.** Daniel is
  frightening because of what he chooses, not because of a condition. Sam is
  called crazy by the town and is the clearest person in it.
- **The insanity background must not be "it was all in his head".** The story
  bible already says so: something specific happened in the missing hours.
- **Ruth's drinking** is how she gets through loneliness and complicity. Write
  it without judgement and without making it a joke.
- **Hope has to be real.** If rebuilding exists in the game, the player has to be
  able to reach it.
- **A content note at the start of the game** (proposal): one quiet screen saying
  the game deals with mental illness, grief, self-harm and isolation. Players
  who need it will be glad of it; it costs nothing.

## Check every scene

Before a scene is done, ask:

- [ ] **Which theme is it carrying?** At least one, without saying its name.
- [ ] **Is someone lonely in it?** Usually yes. Is it a different loneliness
      from the last scene?
- [ ] **Is something left unsaid?** The important thing should rarely be said
      straight out.
- [ ] **Does a choice cost something,** to MC or to someone else?
- [ ] **Is there desire in it?** Even small: someone wanting something they
      shouldn't, or can't have.
- [ ] **If a voice speaks, is it an inner life,** not a gimmick?
- [ ] **Does it leave room for rebuilding,** even if this scene doesn't take it?
- [ ] **Would someone living with these feelings recognise them,** and not feel
      mocked?

## Checking what's written so far (days one and two)

- **Strong:** emptiness, loneliness, fear, guilt, not communicating. Walt's
  name, Cody's ledger line, the phone, the thirteenth stool.
- **Weak:** **desire** is almost absent. **Rebuilding** has its mechanic
  (keeping the rules) but no scene yet where MC feels it. **Meaning** is only
  in Sam so far.
- **Next scenes to fix that:** a first small comfort (`fray=-1`) in day three;
  a moment of desire with Ruth or Sam that isn't romance yet, just wanting to
  be seen; and Daniel's first "sweetheart" on the radio or phone, felt as
  wanting as much as fear.
