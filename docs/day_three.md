# Breakup: Day three

**Friday, October 8, 1999.** Sunrise 8:20, sunset 19:05 (three minutes less
light again). **Built, Oct 12:** `story/day_three.txt`, `night_three.txt`, the
Friday parts of `town.txt`, tested in `tests/test_day_three.gd`. What changed
on the way in is listed under "As built" at the end. Change anything. **Updated Oct 12** to match
`pillars.md` and `daniel.md` v6: two explanations for everything.

Day one taught the counter. Day two brought day one back and showed what the
shop is. **Day three is the first day that's a little bit yours:** you dress
the window and arrange the shelves, the town answers what it sees, and for the
first time somebody is kind to MC. The kindness comes with an undertow.

What `themes.md` asked day three for:

- **A first small comfort** (`fray=-1`). Rebuilding needs a scene where MC
  feels it.
- **Desire:** wanting to be seen. **Ruth** asks what they called MC before
  (decided; Sam asks for the true name later, on day four or after). Not
  romance yet.
- **Daniel, without Daniel:** the first money order, "Interest, ticket 0527",
  postmarked Anchorage. And that night, ??? says "sweetheart" in a cadence MC
  knows. He never speaks to MC until New Year's Eve (`daniel.md`, section 6).
- **Rule 2** ("Don't buy anything that's still warm") came up once, with Walt's
  silver on day one. Today it's harder: the warm thing belongs to a dead man
  the player knows.
- **Rule 8** (the window after midnight) hasn't either. Tonight, with the window
  you dressed.

## Before opening: Friday is window day

On the calendar upstairs, every Friday has the same mark in the owner's hand:
**W.** In the counter drawer, an index card: *"Fridays: dress the window. They
look in on the way to the Gleaner."*

- The front window has **three spots**, seen from the road. Put three floor
  items there, or leave spots empty.
- The town reads the window. **What's in it decides who comes in this
  afternoon** (customer 3 below), and some people comment on it.
- Paranoia, the first time: *"Everybody who drives past will see what you chose.

## Customers

Order: Olstad → the gift → the afternoon caller → the warm jacket.

### 1. Sgt. Olstad: the money order, and the tin

Her second visit. She already touched the tin on day one, at the back shelf.
Today she has a reason to come back to it.

- **She brings the shop's mail:** *"Post office asked me to drop this off,
  hon. Saves them the drive."* Among the circulars, a postal money order. No
  name. *Interest, ticket 0527.* Exact to the cent. Postmarked Anchorage.
  (Daniel keeps the tin from forfeiting; `daniel.md` 6b. MC doesn't know who.)
- She reads it over MC's shoulder, like she had every right. Then she goes
  behind the counter to the back shelf, picks up **the tin box (tag 0527)**,
  shakes it, listens. *"Somebody's paying interest on a cough-drop tin. What's
  in the tin, Johnny?"*
  - Say you don't know (true). Leans nothing; she believes it least of all.
  - *"Something somebody pawned."* (John.) *"Ohh, ya. Somebody."*
  - Show her the ticket. **Dangerous.** She reads the number, hands it back.
    *"Hang onto that, hon."* Sets a flag she remembers in November.
- She looks at the window on her way out and says something about it (one line
  per item). If the window is empty: *"Nothing in the window. Abraham always
  had something in the window."*
- **When she's gone, one thing on the shelves has been moved.** Put it back or
  leave it. (The shelf mechanic carries the scene: she rearranges your shop.)

  MC sits behind the counter for a minute and feels something like warm.

- **Rule 2 plays against it:** the dish is still warm. It isn't for sale and
  isn't being bought, so the rule doesn't apply. Paranoia worries anyway (it

### 4. The warm jacket (rule 2)

Late, near closing. A stranger, or Teague if he came earlier. He has a
**hunting jacket**, waxed canvas, the name stitched inside the collar:
**R. HOLLIS.**

- *"Found it on the north shore. Thought somebody'd want to buy it back."*
- **It's warm.** He says he carried it inside his own coat the whole way,
  against the cold. His hands are bare and blue. It's nine degrees out. (Cody,
  day two: *"it had his jacket."* Two explanations: a stranger who found it, or
  a stranger who took it off someone.)
- Buy it: **breaks rule 2.** The jacket goes on the back shelf. What it does
  later is open (Mrs. Hollis will see it there).
- Refuse: he shrugs and leaves with it. Through the window you watch him walk
  toward the lake, not the road.
- If Mrs. Hollis brought the dish this morning: the dish and the jacket are on
  the counter at the same time, both warm.

## Evening: Friday at the Gleaner

The Gleaner is full on Fridays. The town drinks and doesn't look at the
Keeper's stool. Ruth works the bar alone.

- **Desire, Ruth's version:** after last call she pours two and doesn't put
  the bottle away. She asks the question nobody in town asks the Keeper:
  *"What did they call you, before?"* Not the name: what people called MC.
  - Tell her something true (not the name): a nickname, what a parent called
    MC. **Desire and risk:** wanting to be seen, and being seen. She doesn't
    write it down.
  - *"John."* She looks at the wall of Polaroids. *"That's what they all say."*
- How warm she is depends on day two (the stairs, the bowl).
- The lake and the cabin stay open. If Sam's thread is chosen instead (open
  questions), this scene moves to the ice.

## Night three: the window after midnight (rule 8)

- **The radio** (rule 7): Friday isn't the Thursday watch. Static all night,
  and once, in the static, the calm voice: *"...the windows, friends. Mind the
  windows."*
- **No phone call tonight.** MC notices they're listening for it. The silence
  feels like something missing (`daniel.md`: "the silence should feel like
  loss"). If MC spoke first on night two, this is the punishment: he hangs up,
  and the next night there's no call.
- **??? says "sweetheart"**, once, as MC lies awake, slow and courteous, in a
  cadence MC knows from the night John died. Who learned it from whom is a
  question about MC's mind (`daniel.md` 6c).
- **After midnight** you wake up thirsty. The apartment's water is off (cold
  pipes). Downstairs, the shop. The neon through the window you dressed.
- **In the window,** among the things you chose, your reflection. Faint, laid
  over the snow. It raises its hand. Before you do.
  - Don't wave: rule 8 kept. It keeps its hand up until you look away.
  - Wave back: **breaks rule 8.** The cost is social and psychological, never
    proven (`pillars.md`): a few days later someone in town thanks MC for
    waving from the shop window on a night MC was upstairs asleep (mistaken,
    lying, or something wearing your face). And now and then, the bathroom
    mirror upstairs is half a second late. Later, Daniel waves at his own
    reflection and nothing follows, and the player remembers what followed
    theirs.
- What's in the window changes the scene a little: the fox's glass eyes catch
  the neon; the snowshoes stand up like a person; an empty window has nothing
  between you and it.

## The shop: shelves, the window, examining, customers you can see

The new mechanics (agreed: window draws customers, close-ups with clues). They
work on every day, not just day three. **Built** (first version, playable on
days one and two): `scripts/shelf_view.gd`, `item_art.gd`, `person_art.gd`,
`examine_view.gd`, `story/people.cfg`, `story/examine.txt`, the `shape=` key in
`stock.cfg`, and `if=window:fox`. Not built yet: Olstad moving a thing on the
shelves, and day three's story itself.

### Shelves

- The shop floor's shelves are drawn, with **each object drawn on its spot**
  (placeholder shapes until there's art). The back shelf the same, with tags.
- **Click an object to pick it up; click an empty spot to put it there.** Your
  arrangement stays from day to day (saved).
- The window is three spots of its own, seen from the road.
- While a customer is waiting, the object you're holding can be **handed over**
  (drop it on the counter). That replaces the current "Offer" button.

### The window

- Only floor items (for sale) go in the window.
- `if=window:fox` in a story file: the fox is in the window. Visits and lines
  can depend on it, like everything else.
- What's in the window stays until you change it. Only Fridays are *window
  day* in the story, but you can change it any time before opening.

### Examining

- While a customer waits, click **Examine** on a held object: a **close-up**
  fills the shop side of the screen.
  Things on it can be clicked (the same hotspots as the rooms): open the lid,
  turn it over, read the tag.
- Each click can reveal a **clue** (written in `stock.cfg` / a new
  `examine.cfg`), and clues stay in a small list for that object.
- Customers' requests are written so **the clues decide**, not the
  descriptions (June: "it sang", "her name was in it").
- Voices still comment on what they notice (Appraisal most).

### Customers you can see

- Each person has a **body and a face** drawn as a placeholder: height, build,
  coat colour, hat, hair, skin, eyes, mouth, from a new `story/people.cfg`.
  The art comes later; replacing a placeholder with a painting means adding an
  image, nothing else.
- They stand **across the counter** while they talk, come in with the bell, and
  leave. Small changes by line (`face=worried`, `face=smile`) can come later;
  for now one face each.
- Voices (Paranoia, John...) aren't people and never appear.
