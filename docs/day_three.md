**Friday, October 8, 1999.** Sunrise 8:20, sunset 19:05 (three minutes less
light again). A draft for discussion, the same as `day_two.md` was. Nothing
here is written into the game yet. Change anything.

shop is. **Day three is the first day that's a little bit yours:** you dress
the window and arrange the shelves, the town answers what it sees, and for the
first time somebody is kind to MC. The kindness comes with an undertow.

  romance yet.

## Before opening: Friday is window day

On the calendar upstairs, every Friday has the same mark in the owner's hand:
**W.** In the counter drawer, an index card: *"Fridays: dress the window. They
look in on the way to the Gleaner."*

- The front window has **three spots**, seen from the road. Put three floor
  items there, or leave spots empty.
- The town reads the window. **What's in it decides who comes in this
  afternoon** (customer 3 below), and some people comment on it.
- Paranoia, the first time: *"Everybody who drives past will see what you chose.
  Choose like nobody's looking."*

## Customers

Order: Olstad → the gift → the afternoon caller → the warm jacket.

### 1. Sgt. Olstad: "What's in the tin, Johnny?"

Her second visit. The bible says she handles things that aren't hers. Today
she does it on purpose, behind the counter.

- She goes straight to the back shelf, picks up **the tin box (tag 0527)**,
  shakes it, listens. *"What's in the tin, Johnny?"*
  - Say you don't know (true). Leans nothing; she believes it least of all.
  - *"Something somebody pawned."* (John.) *"Ohh, ya. Somebody."*
  - Show her the ticket. **Dangerous.** She reads the number, hands it back.
    *"Hang onto that, hon."* Sets a flag she remembers in November.
- She looks at the window on her way out and says something about it (one line
  per item). If the window is empty: *"Nothing in the window. Abraham always
  had something in the window."*
- **When she's gone, one thing on the shelves has been moved.** Put it back or
  leave it. (The shelf mechanic carries the scene: she rearranges your shop.)

### 4. The warm jacket (rule 2)

Late, near closing. A stranger, or Teague if he came earlier. He has a
**hunting jacket**, waxed canvas, the name stitched inside the collar:
**R. HOLLIS.**

- *"Found it on the north shore. Thought somebody'd want to buy it back."*
- **It's warm.** Not from him: from inside, the way a coat is warm when
  someone just took it off. It's nine degrees out. (Cody, day two: *"it had his
  jacket."*)
- Buy it: **breaks rule 2.** The jacket goes on the back shelf. What it does
  later is open (Mrs. Hollis will see it there).
- Refuse: he shrugs and leaves with it. Through the window you watch him walk
  toward the lake, not the road.
- If Mrs. Hollis brought the dish this morning: the dish and the jacket are on
  the counter at the same time, both warm.

## The shop: shelves, the window, examining, customers you can see

The new mechanics (agreed: window draws customers, close-ups with clues). They
work on every day, not just day three. **Built** (first version, playable on
days one and two): `scripts/shelf_view.gd`, `item_art.gd`, `person_art.gd`,
`examine_view.gd`, `story/people.cfg`, `story/examine.txt`, the `shape=` key in
`stock.cfg`, and `if=window:fox`. Not built yet: Olstad moving a thing on the
shelves, and day three's story itself.

### Shelves

- The shop floor's shelves are drawn, with **each object drawn on its spot**
  (placeholder shapes until there's art). The back shelf the same, with tags.
- **Click an object to pick it up; click an empty spot to put it there.** Your
  arrangement stays from day to day (saved).
- The window is three spots of its own, seen from the road.
- While a customer is waiting, the object you're holding can be **handed over**
  (drop it on the counter). That replaces the current "Offer" button.

### The window

- Only floor items (for sale) go in the window.
- `if=window:fox` in a story file: the fox is in the window. Visits and lines
  can depend on it, like everything else.
- What's in the window stays until you change it. Only Fridays are *window
  day* in the story, but you can change it any time before opening.

### Examining

- While a customer waits, click **Examine** on a held object: a **close-up**
  fills the shop side of the screen.
  Things on it can be clicked (the same hotspots as the rooms): open the lid,
  turn it over, read the tag.
- Each click can reveal a **clue** (written in `stock.cfg` / a new
  `examine.cfg`), and clues stay in a small list for that object.
- Customers' requests are written so **the clues decide**, not the
  descriptions (June: "it sang", "her name was in it").
- Voices still comment on what they notice (Appraisal most).

### Customers you can see

- Each person has a **body and a face** drawn as a placeholder: height, build,
  coat colour, hat, hair, skin, eyes, mouth, from a new `story/people.cfg`.
  The art comes later; replacing a placeholder with a painting means adding an
  image, nothing else.
- They stand **across the counter** while they talk, come in with the bell, and
  leave. Small changes by line (`face=worried`, `face=smile`) can come later;
  for now one face each.
- Voices (Paranoia, John...) aren't people and never appear.
