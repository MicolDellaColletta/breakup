# Breakup: Daniel v7, a brainstorm

Oct 13. This picks up the notes on Daniel: he's too kind. He's a preacher, not a
priest, and a hard one. Alone with MC he's himself, and enjoys MC's fear. He's
against the town as well as MC. He's a con man running from the law, and he
kills. He lets MC into things if MC seems willing. The endings come down to four
choices: go with him, let the town have MC, escape with someone, or escape
alone (the only true escape, and the hardest). He wants to prove how far he'll
go to have MC. **And a flashback: why MC?**

This is a brainstorm with leans, like the other plans. Once the leans are
agreed, it replaces `daniel.md` v6, and the decisions go to `decisions.md`.

---

## 1. Who he is now

**Before (v6):** a hired killer for the crew who doesn't believe any of it,
always pays and never lies. From his arrival he's a polite stranger who's also
gentle.

**Proposal:** a **con man who kills.** "Daniel Hale" is an alias, just as
"John" is MC's alias. He has worn a dozen names. He has run tent revivals
across the northern plains since the early eighties: healings, collections,
and towns left poorer, with one or two people dead in every one. There are
warrants in three states, under three names, and none of them has his face.

- **The crew job is real, but it's a side job.** The crew hired him to bring
  back John's books because he's good at finding people. The books are the
  reason he came to Anchorage. They're not why he came to Kettle Lake.
- **What he wants is amusement:** proving people will do anything if the
  voice is right. Every town is an experiment. Kettle Lake is the best one he
  has found: a town that already has a ritual, a date and a victim. He only has
  to lean on it.
- **He's against the town.** He despises them more than he despises MC: they
  believe. Alone with MC he makes fun of them, and it's funny, and that's the
  horror.
- **Keep the creed, and make it colder.** **He never lies**, so he tells MC
  exactly what he has done, and it's worse than any lie. **He always pays**: the
  exact change becomes a con man's joke, payment for things that can't be paid
  for (Ezra's hand, the Reverend, Olstad's sleep). Lean: keep both rules.
- **Lean:** a con man who kills, with the hitman job demoted to his cover for
  coming north.

## 2. A preacher, not a priest

He's the son of a Lutheran pastor, gone **revivalist**: tent meetings, altar
calls, hellfire. That isn't a quiet confessional, it's public shame.

| Was (priest-like) | Becomes (preacher) |
|---|---|
| The confession booth in the hall | **The anxious bench:** the front pew, where sinners sit in front of everyone while the hall prays over them out loud. He calls them up by name, and what he knows about them comes from his files on the town. |
| Gentle sermons | **Hellfire.** Leviticus, Jeremiah, Revelation. He shouts. He names sins from the pulpit, with the sinner in the third row. |
| Hearing confessions, keeping the seal | **Testimony:** people confess standing up, in public, and he makes them say it twice. He doesn't keep secrets. He spends them. |
| "Kind" | **Hard.** Fasting, the scale, shaved heads for penance (Ruth's hair, in March?), the town watching the town. |

- **Privately, the counsel room:** a back room behind the hall, one bulb and
  two chairs. That's where he's himself, and where MC is called.
- **Lean:** retexture the booth as the anxious bench (public) plus the counsel
  room (private). It's a rewrite pass on the built hall days (30 and 34) and
  the vestry (December).

## 3. Two Daniels: the pulpit and the room

The town gets the preacher. **Only MC gets the man.** That's the intimacy, and
it's a threat.

**The pulpit:** loud, certain, punishing. *"There is a sinner in this room who
thinks the Lord can't see him in the dark. Stand up, Walt."*

**Alone with MC:**

- He drops the voice in the middle of a sentence. He's quiet, amused, curious,
  and he watches MC's face the way MC watches a scale.
- **He likes the fear.** *"There. That. Do that again."* He enjoys confusing
  MC: he says one true thing in three ways, and lets MC choose which to be
  afraid of.
- He makes fun of the town. *"Mrs. Hollis gave me her husband's wedding ring
  for the plate. I'm wearing it. Look."*
- He never raises his voice, he's too close, he fixes MC's collar. He knows
  MC's real name and doesn't say it, yet.
- He describes what he has done plainly: the Reverend, Ezra's hand. *"You knew.
  I'm only saying it out loud so you can't pretend."*

**What changes in the built scenes:**

- From Thanksgiving to February he has about 120 lines over 18 days. The kind
  ones need rewriting, for example *"That's a kind lie,"* *"You're always kind,
  in the end,"* *"They'll be kind to him. I promise you that."*
- The rule: **in public, hard; with MC alone, himself; never kind.**
- **Lean:** one rewrite pass over every built Daniel scene, as its own PR,
  before March.

## 4. Letting MC in

Daniel's three "doors" (closeness) become **"let in"**. When MC seems
**pliant and interested**, Daniel shows MC something. Each offer is a choice:
lean in, play along, or flinch.

| When | What he lets MC into | Lean in = |
|---|---|---|
| December (after the fire) | **The Reverend.** In the counsel room he tells MC how it went, plainly. Or he gives MC the vestry key: *"Go and look. I left it for you."* | MC knows, and says nothing to Olstad. |
| January | **The files:** his notebooks on every person in town, from his "hot reading" (the homework that lets him "know" people's sins). MC reads them. Then Daniel asks MC to add one: *"You know Ruth better than I do."* | MC writes a line. |
| February | **The collection:** where the money goes. The rings, the deeds and the savings, in a suitcase under his bed above the Gleaner. He offers MC a share, in exact change. | MC takes it. That was MC's first price in 1988 (section 6). |
| March | **The plan for breakup** (section 7). | MC helps. |

- Flinching isn't free: **he loses interest, and a bored Daniel is worse.** He
  goes back to the town, and turns them on MC harder, through the eye.
- **Lean:** yes. Reuse `door=daniel` / `doors:daniel>=n` as the "let in"
  count. Leaning in also costs faith with someone (Olstad, Sam), so going
  along with him is never free.

## 5. How far he'll go

The second motive, under the amusement: **he wants to prove to MC how far he
will go to have him.** He wins MC over by escalating.

1. **November:** he arrives, still bleeding, and pawns the sixth bullet. He
   came 600 miles to look at MC. *(Built.)*
2. **December:** the Reverend, MC's one helper. **He removes him.** *(Built:
   the fire.)*
3. **February:** Ezra's hand. Nobody else gets to call MC the goat.
   *(Built.)*
4. **February:** Olstad asleep in her cruiser, her rounds lined up on the
   dashboard. *(Planned; move it into the rewrite pass.)*
5. **March:** **someone who hurt MC disappears.** Who is the player's doing:
   the person MC has the lowest faith with, or whoever betrayed MC last.
   Exact change on MC's kitchen table the next morning.
6. **April:** **the town itself.** *"They want to give you to the ice. I can
   give them to it instead. All of them. They'd walk out singing if I asked.
   Do you want to see?"*

**Lean:** yes. Step 5 is new (March). Step 6 is the climax of the Daniel
ending (section 7).

## 6. The flashback: why MC

Here are three options. **I lean A.**

### A. The lake, summer 1988 (lean)

Eleven years earlier. MC is seventeen, in a small lake town on the northern
plains. A tent revival comes for the summer. The preacher calls himself
**Brother Abel** (an alias). It's Daniel, at thirty.

- MC's mother gives the tent everything: the savings, then the house.
- One night, after the meeting, MC goes behind the tent and **sees him counting
  the collection and laughing.** Daniel sees MC see him. He doesn't hide. He
  holds out a roll of bills: *"Everyone has a price. What's yours?"*
- **MC takes it.** That's MC's first price, and his first silence.
- **The baptism at the lake:** a girl, or MC's younger brother, held under
  "until the sin comes out." She doesn't come up. The tent folds in the night.
  The town calls it an accident. MC knows what he saw, and was paid not to say.
- **MC leaves home at seventeen and never stops running.** Whatever happened on
  the John night (the four backgrounds), the 1988 lake is under it. MC was a
  runaway long before he became John.

**Why it works:**

- **It explains everything about Daniel's obsession.** MC is the only person
  who ever saw him whole and **chose him**: took the money and kept quiet. A
  witness who was bought is the most intimate thing Daniel has ever had. Then
  MC ran, and Daniel lost him. Every town since has been a little boring.
- **The John night stops being chance, but only halfway.** The crew sends him
  after John. Tracking John, he finds MC with him, and **knows him at once.**
  After that it's no longer about the books.
- **His one lie pays off.** In March (already planned): *"I said I didn't know
  why I let you go. I knew."* The flashback plays that night.
- **His real name:** Daniel knew it in 1988. When he finally says it, it's the
  boy's name, from the lake.
- **Water:** a baptism at a lake in summer, and the ice at breakup. Kettle
  Lake is the second time.
- **The law:** Olstad finds the 1988 drowning in a Minnesota file. A
  description matches "Hale", and a local boy is listed as a witness who left
  town. She works out who MC is before he tells her.
- **It works under every background.** The flashback comes before the John
  night, so it doesn't overwrite any of them. Under **Insanity** it can be
  doubted (*"Or that's what you've decided happened"*).

### B. The John night, from his side

No earlier meeting. The flashback is October 1999 as Daniel saw it: MC's face
in the doorway, and something in it (no fear, or the right fear) stopped him.

- This is closest to v6, but thinner. "He saw something in your eyes" doesn't
  carry *how far he will go*.

### C. His own childhood

Minnesota, the pastor's kitchen. A brother through the ice, and his father
preaching the funeral. MC looks like the brother, or reminds him.

- This explains Daniel, not Daniel-and-MC, and it turns MC into a stand-in.

**How the flashback plays (for A):**

- **What:** a short walked scene in its own look: summer light, the only warm
  colour in the game, the tent, the lake. It comes in three pieces, in
  February, March and the night before breakup.
- **The name:** MC's name is spoken in it, and the sound drops out every time.
- **Where:** the pieces surface from the pawned objects (the hymnal, a tent
  stake in a box lot, a baptism photo in the bear?) and play at night, like
  the John night flashes.

## 9. Darker: a past that won't hold still

**Is v7 dark enough?** His deeds are dark, but his past is too explained: a
con man, warrants, one summer in 1988. An explained man is a man you can
arrest. Darker means **we learn less, and what we learn doesn't agree.** MC is
the one who remembers him, and MC isn't reliable.

**The trick: he never lies, but his stories don't match.** Each time MC asks
about his past in the counsel room, he answers plainly and truthfully, and
each time the answer is different. If he never lies, then **MC is
misremembering**, and the player has to decide which one is broken. The doubt
moves from Daniel onto MC. No other rule in the game does that.

### Fragments, never a biography

Each fragment comes from a different source, and none of them confirms the
others.

| Source | What it says | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Olstad's file** (Feb/Mar) | "Daniel Hale" is on a Minnesota gravestone: a boy, drowned at eight, in 1961, through the ice. **He's wearing a dead child's name.** | The ice was his before it was the town's. |
| **A clipping in the files** (Mar) | 1984, North Dakota: a church in a grain elevator, 31 dead, the preacher never found. No photograph, only a description of his voice. | It sets up April. Ask him and he says: *"Ask me properly."* If MC does, he tells him, and it's worse than the clipping. |
| **His father** (one line, the counsel room) | The pastor was found dead in his church in 1971, with the boy, thirteen, in the front pew, *"still listening."* Nothing more, ever. | It's left to the player. |
| **The cigar box** (his room above the Gleaner, a night walk) | One keepsake from every town: a wedding ring, a lock of hair, a baby tooth, a hymnal page. And one from 1988: **a photograph of MC at seventeen, wet, on a lake shore.** And an empty slot, already labelled in pencil: *Kettle Lake.* | MC was collected. So is the town. |
| **His body** (noticed, never confirmed) | The wound from the sixth bullet is still wet in March. He doesn't eat, ever. One morning at minus thirty, his breath doesn't fog. | That's the gap between strands. MC can't tell if it's real. |

### The 1988 lake changes each time

The flashback plays in three pieces, and each time **it's different, and
worse:**

1. **First:** the tent, the money, the girl at the baptism. Brother Abel holds
   her under. MC watches from the shore.
2. **Second:** the same scene, but MC is standing **in the water**, closer.
   Abel's hand is on MC's shoulder. MC's hands are on the girl.
3. **Third (the night before breakup):** the preacher's face, when he finally
   turns round, is **MC's own.** Or Daniel's, laughing. The player sees
   whichever face fits their background and their strongest voice.

What's true is left to the backgrounds:

- **Guilt:** MC drowned her, and built Daniel to carry it. But Daniel is real
  in the town: he broke Ezra's hand. The town saw.
- **Witness:** all three pieces are true, in order. MC was moved closer each
  time because that's what happened.
- **Debt:** MC owes Daniel for the second piece.
- **Insanity:** there was no tent. Or there was, and MC was never there.
  Whatever the truth, Daniel knows the dream in detail.

**Lean:** yes to all of section 9. It doesn't replace the 1988 lake in
section 6, it decays it. Daniel's lines stay true, and the game never says
which fragment is real.

## 10. The red light

At present only **his phone lines** are red (`speakers.cfg`, January). There's
no light effect yet. Proposal:

- **The scene goes red when he's present.** A dim red cast over the whole
  picture, multiplied in, not a wash. It fades in over about two seconds when
  he enters or speaks, and fades out when he leaves. The lamps take it most:
  the stove glow, the bulb, the candle.
- **Stronger alone:** in the counsel room, or when it's only him and MC, the
  red deepens, and the light pulses very slightly when he enjoys something
  (MC's fear, a flinch).
- **It lies when MC is frayed:** with ??? strong, the red comes **when he
  isn't there.** An empty shop at night, the stove glowing red, nobody in the
  room. The player stops trusting the signal, which is the point.
- **How:** one overlay layer shared by the counter, visit and night scenes.
  It comes up automatically on any `daniel` line (or with a `red=on` /
  `red=off` effect for scenes where he's present but silent) and drops at a
  scene change or when he leaves. It's saved with the scene, and tested.
- **Lean:** yes. Build it with the rewrite pass (section 3), so every built
  Daniel scene gets the light at the same time.
