**Saturday October 9 to Tuesday October 12, 1999.** Agreed on October 12
(the open questions at the end are decided). Day four is being built first.
Checked against `pillars.md`, `forks.md`, `daniel.md`, `underneath.md`.

Sunset keeps the built pace, three minutes earlier a day: Sat 19:02, Sun

## The arc of the four days

Week one is the hook. Days one to three taught the shop, the rules and the
town. These four close the week on **the John night** (hard fork 1,
`forks.md`): the background settles, and the flashes begin.

| Day | Its job | The strand that leads | The comfort | The underneath |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sat 9** | The town's day off. The last thread of day three closes (the jacket); Sam opens his first door. | Psychological | The jacket given back | A braid of hair, pawned for the church |
| **Sun 10** | Church. The town prays for the Keeper, with MC in the room or outside the window. | Folk horror | Mrs. Hollis's kitchen, returning the dish | The weighing; the height mark |
| **Mon 11** | The week closes in. The rental company calls the shop in daylight, asking for John by his surname. | Crime thriller | First real snow | Everyone knocks twice |
| **Tue 12** | The John night. MC alone with John's things, and the revolver. The background settles; the first flash. | All three | None. That's the point | The rhyme, at night |

What carries across all four:

- **The silent calls come back.** Spoke first on night two: silence on nights
  three and four, then the call returns on Sunday night, a breath longer each
  time. Otherwise it rings on nights four, five and seven, never six (Monday).
  He's patient. Never a word (`daniel.md`: he never speaks to MC until New
  Year's Eve).
- **The dog, every night.** Feeding is mercy toward Mrs. Hollis; nothing on
  screen says so. One clue a night (below).
- **The voices.** Each day gives every voice at least one strong choice,
  because the background is worked out from the voices on Tuesday night.
- **The town's eye.** Each day has one moment someone sees MC break a rule or a
  custom, through a watcher (Ezra, Olstad, the bar, the radio).

## Day four: Saturday, October 9

### Morning: night three comes back

| Last night | This morning |
|---|---|
| Waved back at the reflection | The bathroom mirror is **half a second late.** MC lifts a hand to shave; the face in the glass lifts its hand just after. Once. Paranoia: *"You're tired. Your eyes are tired."* (`day_three.md`: the cost of waving, never proven.) |
| Didn't wave | Nothing in the mirror. MC checks it anyway, twice. |
| Fed the dog, set the bowl off the line (Sam's test) | **The bowl is back on the line. Exactly.** Either someone watches MC very closely, or MC got up in the night and fixed it. Sam's test answered nothing, which is an answer. |
| Fed, no test | The bowl licked clean, moved, and a hairpin in it. The old kind, bronze, the kind women use for a bun. |
| Didn't feed | On the bottom stair, two round marks in the dust of the tread, side by side, a hand's width apart. Knees. |
| Stayed in bed (no water) | The bell over the front door has a strip of tape wound round its clapper. MC didn't do that. Somebody wanted to come in quietly. |

### Customers

**1. The jacket comes home.** Depends on day three:

| If | Then |
|---|---|
| MC set the jacket aside for her (`faith_hollis`) | **Mrs. Hollis**, come for nothing in particular. MC gives her the jacket. She holds it to her face. *"It's warm."* It isn't; it's been on a shelf all night. She takes it without asking where it came from. **Kept faith with the Hollises.** |
| The jacket is on the back shelf, tagged | **Cody** (if alive) sees it from the counter: *"Where'd you get that?"* He wants it; he hasn't got ten dollars. Sell it, give it, or keep it. Selling breaks faith. |
| MC refused the stranger | Mrs. Hollis, for nothing. She mentions a man walking the north shore at dawn with no gloves. *"He waved."* |

**2. Something for the church.** **Hanna Lund**, sixteen, Ezra's
granddaughter, with a braid of fair hair in a bread bag. Cut this morning. She
wants it written in the book and held: *"For the church, when asked. Grandpa
says you'll know how."* No loan.
- **Rule 3, every item gets a price.** The ledger needs one. MC prices a
  child's hair, or leaves it blank and breaks the rule.
- She isn't upset. She's proud. Her neck is bare and red with cold, and she
  touches it.
- Appraisal can find the jar of milk teeth on the same shelf: same ledger
  words. Paranoia: *"When asked. Asked for what."*
- The town's eye: Ezra hears what MC did, either way.

**3. Sam, at the counter.** The first time he comes into the shop. He buys
batteries and stays. **Sam's first door** (`forks.md`): he puts his notebook on
the glass, open, and MC sees the line already started: *October 5, 1999.
"John". ___* *"I start them in October. It saves time in May."* He asks for
MC's real name, *"so the right one's written down."*
- Give it (door 1, `door_sam_1`). He writes it in pencil and doesn't say it
  aloud. Then asks MC to help with the others: who were the ten?
- *"John."* He writes nothing. *"Then I'll leave it blank."*
- Take the pencil and cross out "John". Animal; a different door: he looks at
  MC as if for the first time.
- Ruth opened her door yesterday (if she did). **Two people now hold
  something true about MC.** Neither knows about the other.

**4. Nobody.** Late afternoon, Saturday quiet. The phone rings once, in
daylight, and stops. Daylight calls are new.

### Evening

- **The Gleaner on a Saturday:** pool, a fight that doesn't happen, Olstad off
  duty and drinking milk, watching. Ruth is busy. If MC opened her door
  yesterday, she slides a beer down the bar with something written on the
  napkin under it: the word MC told her. Nobody else sees. (Being kept.)
- **Sam's cabin** (if door 1 is open): he shows MC the notebook's ten names.
  None of them saw May.
- **The lake:** the first people on the ice, close in, kids sliding. A woman
  calls them back, sharply, in the middle of a game.

### Night four

- **The radio:** Saturday request hour. Someone requests a hymn "for the
  Keeper, who's settling in." The number is 3: rule three, the price.
- **The dog:** fed, the stairs at three, and a sound MC can't place: something
  set down carefully on each step. Knees. Unfed: nothing comes, and in the
  morning (Sunday) the bowl is gone.
- **The phone:** rings at eleven (unless MC spoke first on night two). The open
  line, the clock ticking in the room at the other end. Longer than before.

## Day five: Sunday, October 10

**The shop is closed on Sundays** (Abraham's sign on the door, "Closed for the
Lord's day. Ring if it's the Lord"). Day five has no counter. It's a different
shape: morning, church or not, afternoon, evening, night.

### Morning

- The bowl (unfed last night): **gone.** It's on the church steps when MC
  arrives, upside down. Or, if MC doesn't go to church, on the shop's front step
  at noon, full, the kibble wet with snowmelt.
- Under the door: the church bulletin for today. The prayer list printed at the
  bottom: *Our Keeper. The Hollis boys. The window.*

### Church, or not (the set piece)

**If MC goes** (and Ezra will expect MC if MC promised, `promised_sunday`):

- The whole town. MC is sat at the front, in a pew with nobody else in it.
- The Reverend preaches on **the stranger at the gate** (hospitality,
  Hebrews 13:2), gently, looking at MC. It's a warning in a welcome's clothes.
- **We pray for the Keeper.** The congregation stands and comes forward and
  lays hands on MC, dozens of them, on the shoulders, the back, the head, the
  arms. Warm hands, cold hands. Nobody speaks. It goes on too long. Mrs.
  Hollis's hand is on the back of MC's neck, and her knuckles are rough.
  Paranoia, under it all: *"Count the hands. Count the marks."* Choices: bear
  it (the town's eye: a good Keeper), or stand up and walk out (the town's eye:
  the Keeper wouldn't be prayed for).
- **After, in the vestry:** Ezra and two women. *"For the records."* The old
  grain scale. They weigh MC, write the number in a book with a page for every
  winter. Then they stand MC against the doorframe and pencil a line at the top
  of MC's head, and a name: JOHN. Above and below it, ten other lines, ten
  other names. (`underneath.md`.) MC can refuse the scale; the doorframe
  happens anyway, Ezra quick with the pencil.
- The Reverend at the door, shaking hands, low: *"I told you to be busy."*

**If MC doesn't go:**

- At eleven-thirty, the congregation walks down the hill after the service. All
  of it. They stop in the road outside the shop and stand facing the window,
  and pray. Forty people, silent, hands folded. Ezra at the front. It lasts
  five minutes on the clock and feels like an hour.
- MC can watch from behind the counter, go upstairs and pull the curtain, or
  go out to them. Going out: they part for MC like a door. Mrs. Hollis takes
  MC's hand and holds it, warm, and lets go.
- If MC promised and didn't come: Ezra writes something in a small book,
  standing in the road. The town's eye.

### Afternoon: the dish

- Return Mrs. Hollis's dish (if she brought it). Her house: Ray's boots by the
  door, his chair, his calendar still on September. She's kind, she feeds MC
  again (the comfort, `fray=-1`).
- Things to notice, never pointed at: the floor by her chair is bare and
  scrubbed in two round patches. A dog's bowl, upside down, on the drying rack,
  though there's no dog. A hairpin like the one in MC's bowl.
- If Cody went under: his room's door is shut, and she talks to it once,
  passing, as if he were in.

### Evening

Sunday: the Gleaner is closed. **Ruth's house**, if MC knows where it is (her
door is open after day three): she's drinking alone, the TV on with the sound
off. She doesn't ask MC in, then does. Nothing happens. She falls asleep in
her chair, and MC sees on the shelf a shoebox labelled with years, 1989 to
1998. (Her cellar copies are at the bar; this is something else. Open.)

### Night five

- The radio: the Sunday hymn hour, actual hymns, nothing in them. MC finds the
  quiet harder than the numbers.
- The phone rings (even if MC spoke first on night two: the punishment is
  over). The open line. MC hears, very faintly, a hymn on a radio at the other
  end. The same hymn MC's radio is playing, a second behind.
- ??? (if frayed): *"He's listening to you listen, sweetheart."*

## Day six: Monday, October 11

**The week closes in.** The crime thriller leads. It's the last ordinary day
before the flashes start.

### Morning

- **First real snow** overnight, a foot of it, soft, everything quiet and
  blue. The comfort of the week: MC shovels the walk, and for twenty minutes
  it's just work and breath and a shovel (`fray=-1` if MC does it).
- In the fresh snow, from the back door to the tree line across the road: one
  set of footprints. Going out. Not coming back. (A congregant after feeding
  the dog, going home a long way round. Or MC.)

### Customers

**1. The rental company, by phone, in daylight.** Northern Lights Auto Rental,
Anchorage. A woman, bored, asking for **Mr. John [surname]**. The surname is
the one smudged on every document (`story.md`, "John"). *"Just confirming the
vehicle's return date, sir. It's blank on our copy."*
- Answer as John, give a date. Leans John. Gives the plate a date to be
  overdue (Oct 30, `forks.md` fork 2).
- *"Wrong number."* Leans Paranoia. She calls back.
- Hang up. Animal. The phone rings again an hour later, and nobody's there.
  Was that her, or the other caller? MC can't tell anymore.
- Olstad is in the shop, at the back shelf, while the phone rings. (Order the
  visits so she's there for the call.)

**2. Olstad.** Not for the tin this time. She's looking at the bear.
*"Teague says it was opened twice."* (if Teague came). Or: *"Abraham ever tell
you about the bear?"* She asks who MC is renting from. She knows. She wants to
hear MC say it. Her homophobia, subtly but clearly: about Thomas and his
"friend", a tin of photographs she found after (`pillars.md`). Never a joke.

**3. Ezra, asking.** He comes for the jar of milk teeth. One tooth. He knows
which: the paper says H.L. '89 (Hanna). *"For the church. When asked."* MC can
give it (the custom; the town's eye sees a good Keeper), or refuse (the ledger
says *held*; rule? none). It's the first time MC sees what "when asked"
means, and doesn't learn what it's for.

**4. Everyone knocks twice.** Every customer today knocks twice on the door
frame on the way out. MC notices it on the fourth. Then can't stop noticing.

### Evening

- **The lake:** the poles. Men are taking the tarp off and checking the
  lashings. *"Not yet. December, if it's cold."* One of them looks at MC and
  touches his wrist without meaning to.
- **The town line:** MC can drive to it (the first chance to try to run). The
  sign. Olstad's cruiser in the turnout, engine running, lights off. She lifts
  two fingers off the wheel. MC turns around. (Each attempt is a scene, never a
  game over, `story.md`.)

### Night six

- No phone. Monday: never.
- The dog, and the clue: fed, MC hears it eat. Small, careful sounds. And then,
  once, a word, very soft, from the kitchen. Maybe *"there"*. Maybe a breath.
- The locked door at the end of the hall (Ruth's room): a line of light under
  it, for the first time. Off again before MC reaches it.

## Day seven: Tuesday, October 12. The John night

**Hard fork 1.** The background settles (`settle=background`, built). Short
day, long night.

### The day

- One customer only, at noon: **Walt** (if MC refused him kindly) or **the
  boy** with more fry bread. Small, ordinary, warm. The last ordinary thing.
- **The envelope is late.** It's Tuesday; it comes Wednesdays. But in the mail
  slot at closing: a church envelope, empty, with JOHN written on it in pencil.
  Then crossed out. Nothing written instead.
- **The ledger:** a new line in the owner's hand, under MC's last: *Oct 12. One
  week.* (Anyone with a key; `pillars.md`.)

### The night (the fork)

- MC can't sleep. In the glovebox of the rental, under the maps: **John's
  revolver.** MC brings it up to the kitchen table. Five rounds in six chambers
  (`daniel.md`). The empty one turns under MC's thumb.
- The radio, the static, and the Reverend, unscheduled, late: *"For anyone
  awake tonight. The Lord knows your name, friend. Even if you've put it down
  somewhere."*
- **What MC does with the gun** is the last choice of the week, and it leans
  the last point before the background settles:
  - Put it under the pillow (Animal).
  - Wrap it in a flannel shirt and hide it at the back of the dresser, with
    the bulletin and the rental papers, the way you hide evidence (Appraisal:
    *witness*).
  - Take it outside and push it deep into the snowbank by the back door, where
    it'll be till May (Warmth: *guilt*, you can't stand to have it near).
  - Load the sixth chamber from the box of .22s in the drawer. They don't fit.
    Try anyway (John: *debt*, MC is becoming the man who owned it).
  - Sit with it until the static clears, and listen to what ??? says about it
    (???: *insanity*).
- **`settle=background`.** Then the first flash, per background (`daniel.md`
  6d): a few lines of the night John died, in present tense, Daniel in it and
  never his face. Each flash ends on the same sound: a revolver's cylinder
  turning, one click.
- **The dog** comes up the stairs at three, fed or not, for the first time not
  stopping at the kitchen. It stops outside the bedroom door. It breathes. Then
  it goes down. (Mrs. Hollis, come to look at the Keeper the night he became
  one. Or MC, standing outside his own door.)
- Morning: Wednesday, the envelope, a hundred and fifty dollars, and the second
  week. (The bridge to the month's key days, `pillars.md`, "The calendar".)
