# Breakup: Pillars

The one page every scene, character and system gets checked against. When
another doc disagrees with this one, this one wins, and the other doc gets
fixed. Decided together on October 12; change it on purpose, not by drift.

---

## The game in one breath

> A man running from a death he may have caused takes a winter job under a
> dead man's name, keeping a pawn shop in an Alaska town that believes strange
> things. People disappear, and the town has rules for it. Then a killer
> arrives who believes in nothing, and finds the perfect place to hide. He
> makes himself the town's preacher, and turns its fear into faith, its faith
> into frenzy, and its frenzy toward the stranger in the pawn shop.

Kettle Lake, Alaska. October 1999 to breakup, April 2000.

## The question

**Is it real?** Every strand gives a different answer to the same events, and
the game never settles which one is true.

| Strand | Its answer | Who drives it | What it's afraid of |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crime thriller** | *It's men.* Money, a dead thief, a hired killer. | Daniel, the crew John robbed, Olstad | The unpredictable: a man who decides when |
| **Folk and religious horror** | *It's the town.* Whether or not anything is under the ice, they'll walk you to it. | The congregation, Ezra, the rules, the Keeper legend | The inevitable: a date nobody can move |
| **Psychological horror** | *It's you.* Guilt, paranoia, voices, a mind under strain. | MC's voices, fraying, reflections, the background | The unreliable: you can't trust what you saw |

The **cosmic layer** (what's under the ice) is not a fourth strand. It's what
lives in the gap between the three answers. The game never confirms it.

## The spine: belief, and the abuse of belief

- **The town believes,** and gives a stranger to the ice every winter.
- **MC half-believes,** and obeys eight rules because they might be true.
- **Daniel believes nothing,** and uses everyone else's belief.

Everything else hangs off this: urban legend (the Keeper, the rules, the
names), religious horror grown out of folklore (prayers for the Keeper, the
scapegoat), paranoia (MC's and the town's), mental illness (written with care,
see `themes.md`), and dark romance (wanting the thing that could destroy you).

## The rule of two explanations

**Every uncanny thing has a plausible human or psychological explanation,** at
least until the last scene, and often after it. If something can only be
magic, it gets rewritten.

| Thing | The human explanation |
|---|---|
| The dog, the bowl, the stairs at night | Someone with a key. Or MC, sleepwalking. |
| The ledger line in the dead owner's hand | Anyone with a key, watching the new Keeper. |
| The bear with the old owner inside | Abraham did it, with his own hands, in 1959. |
| Sam's clock stopping at the same minute | His data could be wrong. He could be wrong. |
| The reflection that waves first | Exhaustion, dark glass, a mind under strain. |
| **A pawned name** (Ida) | **A custom.** The town refuses to say a pawned name. Walt can't find it because he's eighty and grieving. People, not magic. |
| **Management's envelopes** | *Open:* who delivers them, and how. It needs a human answer. |

**One ending is allowed to lean supernatural** (see "Endings"), and even that
one can be read as MC losing his mind completely.

## The romances: one per strand

The dark romance is getting close to a threat. Each strand has one.

| Strand | The threat | The romance | What loving them means |
|---|---|---|---|
| Crime thriller | Daniel | **Daniel** | Falling for what you ran from |
| Folk horror | The congregation | **Ruth**, who has helped them every winter | Loving someone complicit |
| Psychological | MC's own mind | **Sam**, as paranoid as MC | Being believed, or sharing a delusion |

The real name runs through all three: Ruth asks what they called him before;
Sam asks for a true name; Daniel knows it and never says it.

## The people who hold the line

- **Daniel Hale** (`daniel.md`): a hired killer, Landa's courtesy and
  Chigurh's principle. A loner, well spoken, scarily polite, armed. He always
  pays, never lies, has no loyalties. He isn't tied to the shop. He's tied to
  the town, because he feeds on what it believes.
- **Sgt. Marit Olstad: good is not the same as nice.** Unpleasant, rude in a
  polite way, and **homophobic, subtly but clearly**: in what she says about
  other people, in who she assumes things about, never as a joke and never
  excused. She doesn't like MC. She still won't let a town drown a man, because
  the law is the only thing she believes in. She's the one person in Kettle
  Lake who believes in neither the ice, nor God, nor Daniel. **She lives.** At
  breakup she's on the shore, against all of them. Protecting MC doesn't
  redeem her prejudice, and the game doesn't pretend it does. (MC's own
  gender is never stated outright; her prejudice shows around him, not in a
  label.)
- **The Reverend Elias**: the calm voice on the night radio, the helper. The
  town turns on him in December.
- **Ezra Lund**: what the congregation sounds like before Daniel gives it a
  voice.

## How the town falls: the spiral

**The millennium is the hinge.** In 1999 the town is already waiting for the
end of the world. Midnight on December 31 comes, and nothing happens. A
disappointed apocalypse looks for a new date (the Millerites, 1844). Daniel
gives them one: *"The end didn't come because the Lord is waiting. For the ice.
For the offering."*

**Daniel's voice arrives before he does,** through the night radio: a caller,
then a guest, then the host. He talks to the whole town every night, and never
to MC. **His wound helps the con:** a preacher with a wound in his side. He
never claims anything; they decide what it means.

**What he gets from it:** access (a town that loves him hands him the shop,
the tin, the Keeper, with no questions), cover (nobody investigates the
preacher, except Olstad), and MC (in a town that wants MC dead, he decides).
And he enjoys it: the pulpit he despises, done better than his father did.

**MC's mind and the town mirror each other.** In October MC is the paranoid one
in a calm town: is he ill? By February the whole town is in a religious frenzy,
and MC's paranoia looks like the only sane response: was he right? By April
nobody, the player included, can tell who's mad.

| Month | The town | Daniel | MC |
|---|---|---|---|
| **October** | Quiet superstition. Rules, prayers for the Keeper, things nobody says. | Silent calls. A money order: "Interest, ticket 0527." | New, frightened, obeying. The voices. |
| **November** | The Reverend's millennium sermons. People stock up. | A caller on the night radio. Then he arrives, wounded, "passing through". A guest on the Thursday watch. | Learning the shop. First warmth (Ruth, Sam). |
| **December** | Y2K fever. The congregation turns on the Reverend. | He tells Ezra one true thing. He takes the microphone. | The helper's voice is gone. |
| **January** | The Disappointment. Then the new date: breakup. | *"Happy New Year, sweetheart."* Preacher and confessor. | Hunted by a man, watched by a town. |
| **February** | Sermons on the scapegoat (Leviticus 16). The Keeper spoken of openly. | Ezra's hand. His duel with Olstad. The open back door. | Paranoia starts to look like sense. |
| **March** | Frenzy. Processions on the ice. | Names his price: the books. | Who to trust: Ruth, Sam, Daniel, or ???. |
| **April** | Breakup. The town walks to the shore. | The shore, holding MC's arm. | The ending. |

## Endings

One per strand, plus the default and the way out.

| Ending | Strand | In short |
|---|---|---|
| **The season ends** (default) | Folk horror | The town gets its Keeper. |
| **Run** | Crime thriller | MC drives away; the shop takes whoever holds the keys. |
| **Daniel** | Crime thriller, romance | At the shore, he doesn't let go. |
| **Break the cycle** (Ruth) | Folk horror, romance | Her complicity undone, at a price. |
| **The truth** (Sam) | Psychological, romance | The rational answer: it was always the town. |
| **The ice** (???) | Psychological / cosmic | MC walks out and meets what's under it. Supernatural, or MC completely gone. The game never says which. Daniel's "into the ice together" is a variant of this one. |

Daniel's four shore outcomes (`daniel.md`, 10b) are how his strand closes
inside these endings, not separate endings.

## The calendar: chapters by month

Every day of a seven-month winter is too much for one person to make.

- **Week one day by day** (October 6–12): the tutorial and the hook.
- **Then four or five key days a month,** about 30 playable days in all, with
  a short bridge between them: the week's envelope, what changed on the
  shelves, what the radio said.
- **Fixed days:** Halloween (the clocks fall back), Thanksgiving, Daniel's
  arrival, Christmas, New Year's Eve, Olstad's duel, breakup.
- The pace is part of the story: October is slow and close, March goes by in a
  blur, the way a long winter does.
