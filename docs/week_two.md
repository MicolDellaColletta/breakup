## What changes after week one

Week one was played day by day. From here the calendar is **key days with
bridges between them** (`pillars.md`, "The calendar"): four or five played
days a month, about 30 in all.

- **A key day** is a full day: morning, the counter (or a walk-around day like
  Sunday), the evening map, the night.
- **A bridge** covers the days in between in under a minute: what the week
  did while you weren't looking.

The pace is part of the story: October is slow and close, so it gets the most
key days after week one. Week two has **two**.

## Week two's job

Week one asked *what is this town?* Week two answers part of it, and the
answer is worse. **The routines become rituals.** The second Thursday, the
second Sunday, the second envelope: MC starts doing things the way the last
Keeper did them, without being told. It's a comfort, and the comfort is the
trap.

- **The underneath gets an answer:** what the hair, the teeth, the weighing and
  the doorframe are *for* (the coat, below).
- **The thriller tightens a notch:** the calls change. A kettle, a pill bottle,
  a breath that catches. He's hurt (`daniel.md`, 6a, mid-October).
- **The psychological strand:** the second flash. And MC's notebook (Sam's),
  if MC writes in it, starts to disagree with what MC remembers.
- **The romances:** no new doors this week (Ruth's second is November, Sam's
  December). Instead, **Ruth and Sam collide over MC** (below): a soft fork
  that costs faith with one of them.

## The coat (proposal: the answer to the underneath)

**The congregation is sewing the Keeper a coat.** A long, heavy wool coat,
made new every winter, by the Ladies' Circle, Thursdays at the Lunds'.

- **The weighing and the doorframe are for the fitting.** Height for the
  length, weight for the cut. *"He filled out by Christmas"*: they let it out.
- **The lining is the town.** Every girl who turns sixteen sews in a braid.
  Every child's first tooth goes in a little pocket, stitched shut. A
  hairpin from every widow. The Keeper carries the whole town's children,
  their sins and their bad luck, out onto the ice at breakup, and leaves them
  there. **Leviticus 16, the scapegoat, sewn into a coat.**
- **It's kind.** The women sew it with love. It's warm. It's the best coat in
  Kettle Lake. The Keeper is meant to be warm, on the walk.
- **The two explanations:** it's a coat. A folk custom, a quilt with a story.
  Nothing happens because of it. That's what makes it unbearable.
- **Where MC sees it:** Thursday evening through the Lunds' window (key day 8);
  the fitting comes later (Thanksgiving or December, open).
- Hanna's braid and the tooth Ezra asked for go into it. If MC refused the
  tooth, it's "asked again" this Sunday, in front of everyone.

## Key day 8: Thursday, October 14. The second Thursday

### Morning

- The second flash's aftertaste: MC wakes knowing one more detail of the John
  night than they did, per background, and doesn't know where it came from.
- **The notebook** (if MC has it, and wrote the date on Monday): today's page
  has a line on it, in MC's handwriting. Something MC doesn't remember
  writing. (`forks.md`, the blur: early, small, deniable.)

### The counter

1. **Ezra, the usual.** The second Thursday. MC knows where the shells are now.
   The ritual completes itself; Ezra's shoulders let go. If MC refused the
   tooth on Monday, he buys the shells without a word and looks at the jar the
   whole time.
2. **Mrs. Hollis, with a dish.** Every week now (the gift became a custom).
   Fattening. *"You've lost weight since Sunday."* She hasn't weighed MC. She
   knows anyway.
3. **Cody,** if alive and the ice picks are held: he wants them back, for
   nothing, because he's going out again. He says he saw something under the
   ice and has to know. Rule? None. Giving them back: kept faith with the
   Hollises, and Cody goes out on thin ice with them. Keeping them: he goes
   anyway, without.
4. **June,** with the children's drawings for the pageant, wanting one pinned
   in the window: crayon, a figure in a long brown coat walking onto blue
   ice, the whole town small and waving on the shore. *"The Keeper's coat,"* she
   says, pleased. *"The children love the coat."*

### Evening

- **The Lunds' house** (new place, Thursdays): through the front window, the
  Ladies' Circle. Eight women at a long table, sewing one enormous brown wool
  coat. Hanna among them, stitching her braid into the lining. Mrs. Hollis
  sewing a hairpin into a cuff.
  - Watch from the road. Someone sees you, and waves, and they all wave.
  - Knock. They let you in, delighted. They hold the coat up to you. *"Not yet.
    Not till it's done."* Someone measures your arm with a tape, for the
    sleeve, before you can say no.
- **The Gleaner**, Thursday: if MC has Sam's door open, **the collision**:
  Sam asks MC to photograph Ruth's wall of Polaroids for him (he has names
  and dates but not faces). Ruth catches MC with the camera:
  - Tell her it's for Sam: she takes the camera, and her face closes. Broken
    faith with Ruth (she knows what Sam does with faces).
  - Lie: she believes you, or pretends to. Broken faith with Sam, later, when
    he learns you didn't bring them.
  - Put the camera away before she sees: no faith broken, and nothing gained.
- **The church:** the Thursday watch. If MC sat in on week one, Ezra pours the
  coffee without asking.

### Night

- **The Thursday broadcast:** the hymn numbers are rules again, but different
  ones. One of them is a number there's no rule for. *"Number nine."* (The
  rhyme: nine's the Keeper.)
- **The call:** a kettle coming to the boil, a pill bottle shaken, a breath
  that catches when he moves. He's hurt. MC can't know that's what it means,
  only that it's new.
- **The dog**, and a clue: something left beside the bowl. A thread of brown
  wool, the coat's wool.

## Key day 9: Sunday, October 17. The second Sunday

Walk-around, like day five (`visit.tscn`). Shorter: the shape is familiar, and
that familiarity is the horror.

- **Church:** MC's pew is the front one again; it's become *MC's* pew.
  - The weighing, again (`weighed`, or refused again: they guess again).
    The number's read out this time, quietly, to the women from Thursday.
  - **If MC refused the tooth:** after the service, Ezra asks again, at the
    door, in front of the whole congregation. *"H.L. Eighty-nine. It's asked."*
    Saying no in front of everyone is the town's eye at its sharpest.
  - **The coat is never mentioned in church.** Nobody says the word.
- **Lunch at Mrs. Hollis's again.** The same roast. The same chair. She's set
  a place for MC that's clearly been MC's place for a week. A ritual already.
- **The comfort, and the cage:** `fray=-1` again, but the voices notice this
  time. Warmth: *"Same time next week. You like it."* Paranoia: *"You like it.
  That's how they do it."*
- **Evening:** Ruth's house again (if not broken), or Sam's, never both: the
  collision decided who's still speaking to MC.

## What has to be built

- **Bridges:** a short scene type, the title card and a few lines, maybe one
  choice, then on to the next key day. `GameState` needs to jump days (today
  `day` counts up by one).
- **Day numbers vs dates:** key day 8 is October 14; the date and the sunset
  have to come from a calendar, not from `day`.
- **The Lunds' house** (new place, Thursday evenings).
- **The coat** as a recurring object (a flag-driven description; later a
  fitting scene).
- **Week two's stories:** two key days, two nights, three bridges.
