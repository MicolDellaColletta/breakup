April 21, 1989.

Tomorrow I'm driving him. Dad's truck, after midnight, while they're all at the church for the watch. Nobody watches the truck.

Mom knows. I didn't tell her. She just knows, the way she always knows. She held my face in the kitchen and said, "Then I'll go and sit in the shop, so it's not empty. Nobody will know till morning."

I said don't be silly. She said somebody has to be at the table.

I love him. I love him. I love him.

(The next page is blank. The page after that, in the same hand, older, in pencil: "She was right. Somebody has to be at the table.")
